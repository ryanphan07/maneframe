- Cai qing (choy ching): "plucking the greens"; the lion approaches a hanging head of lettuce and red envelope, "eats" it, and spits the leaves back out as a blessing.
- Jong (high poles): steel poles of different heights; the lions leap from pole to pole in a show of balance and teamwork.
- Eye-dotting: a ceremony that "wakes" a new lion by dotting its eyes with red paint, usually with a brush.
- Horse stance: the low, wide, planted stance that the head and tail dancers hold; leg strength shows in how long they stay low.
- Head dancer: controls the head, blinks the eyes, wiggles the ears and opens the mouth; also leads the moves.
- Tail dancer: bends at the waist to be the lion's back legs and lifts the head dancer into stunts.
- Drum: sets the tempo and leads the dance; the lion follows its rhythm, and it speeds up for excitement.
- Gong: keeps a steady strike that carries the beat; usually the louder, sharper accent in the band.
- Cymbals: crash with the drum on the big moves and keep the energy up in between.
- Sleeping lion: the lion lies down or curls up, still and quiet, often with a snoring-like head droop.
- Waking lion: the lion stirs, stretches, shakes its head, blinks and then scratches or looks around as if curious.
- Big Head Buddha (Ong Dia): a dancer in a smiling mask with a fan who teases and plays with the lion. Use the name, Ong Dia, when referring to them.
- Lettuce (choy): the greens in cai qing; symbolizes growth and fortune, which the lion eats and spits out.
- Red envelope (lai see): money in a red packet tied to the greens as the reward.
- Bowing: the lion lowers its head to the audience or an altar in a gesture of respect and thanks.
- Southern lion: the Cantonese-style lion with a movable mouth, blinking eyes and a colorful body, as opposed to the northern lion with shaggy fur.

Moves:
- Super snaps (snaps for short): typically done in 5, the lion head "snaps" left and right. the lion is looking around.
- Non-stunt jumps: typically done in 2, the lion does gallop-hops (either in place, or in a direction). can be used after a stunt as a "reset hop"
- 6 looks (triangles): the lion inspects an object, looking in the shape of a triangle. typically done: left down, middle down, middle up. right down, middle down, middle up. 
- 3 blinks: the lion looks around and continues inspecting a certain object. typically done left, right, center. but this can be done in any order.
- Heartbeats: the lion steps left, right, left, right, etc. the lion is waiting for or anticipating something. can be done in place or while moving into a different formation/position
- 7-star (chat sing): a celebratory move. can be done in many different ways, but the most common way for this team is:
    - snap left with right leg following the head, moving to the left behind the left leg.
    - snap right with left leg following the head, moving to the right behind the right leg. 
    - reset towards the middle.
    - 3 super snaps: left, right, middle.
    - head spin: lion head swoops around, down and, back up around in a counter clockwise circle (the performer's left side, down, right side, middle)
    - reset back into strong horse stance with lion looking at audience/object in front of it
- bows: done in sets of 3. done to show respect, and part of every routine. high level idea: lion walks forward (as if entering a king's palace), looks left and right, then bow head down and walk backwards. 
    - lion chambers their right leg into crane stance facing 30-45degrees towards stage left
    - walk forward during drum roll
    - go into horse stance and do 2 super snaps
    - faces head down and retreats for a few steps
    - lion chambers their right leg into crane stance facing 30-45degrees towards stage right
    - walk forward during drum roll
    - go into horse stance and do 2 super snaps
    - faces head down and retreats for a few steps
    - lion chambers their right leg into crane stance directly facing stage center
    - walk forward during drum roll
    - go into horse stance and do 2 super snaps
    - faces head down and retreats for a few steps

Stunts:
- stunts are done with the base holding the head's sash, which should be tied under their belly button and a bit above their hip bones. the base shakes
    the head's sash, then they jump down into a "pre-hop" to get momentum and sync up timing, then the head jumps up as high as they can while the 
    base lifts them up. The base should get as close to the head as possible without bumping into them during the "pre-hop", then wait until the head jumps 
    a bit to where the base's arms are roughly at their chest, then push up as much as possible. On the way down, bases should slightly slow down the momentum
    of the head, making sure not to slam them down. The head should keep their back straight and lean back a bit on the way up, while tucking their knees up to
    their chest and making sure not to kick their legs back and accidentally hit their base.
- Jumps:
    - practice jump: head jumps up as high as they can while the base lifts them as high as they can. the head tucks in their legs (balls up) on the way up.
    - Kick out: practice jump where the head kicks their legs out as the peak of their jump.
    - clap kick: practice jump where the head kicks their legs inward like a clap at the peak of their jump.
    - yoshi: Practice jump where the base moves forward by a few steps, making the entire lion end in a position a few steps ahead of where it started.
- Stacks:
    - Half stack (two legs or two): head jumps and lands on base's legs, who will be in a horse stance. they should land on the base's front hips.
        - this is typically done from a practice jump where the head gets high enough to where their sash is roughly at the base's chin, then the head keeps their legs balled up while the base pulls them onto their chest and holds them
        there. then, the head brings their legs down from their balled-up position and stands on the base's hips.
    - 1 leg: head jumps and lands on one of the base's legs with their matching leg, with the other leg bent in the air (in a crane stance)
        - this is typically done from a practice jump where the head gets high enough to where their sash is above the base's head (ideally high enough where the base's
        arms lock out), and then the base floats the head, then sets into their horse stance and lowers the head onto their leg. 
    - head set: head jumps and lands sitting on the base's head. the head should be sitting slightly leaning back around 30 degrees, with their core tight. the head
        should have one leg on the base's chest, slightly on/above the base's breast on that side. the other foot should be bent in the air, able to kick back and forth. 
        - this is typically done from a practice jump where the head jumps over and clears the base's head, then the base slowly lowers them onto their head.
    - full stack: start from a head set. the head brings their leg that's not on the base's chest towards the base's shoulder on that side. The base 
        positions that leg to be flat on their shoulder. Then the head brings their lead from the chest onto the base's shoulder on that side. The base 
        positions that leg to be flat on their shoulder. then, the head stands up on the base's shoulders.

Routine:
- Typically consists of:
    - Hong Kong wake up: lions are sleeping and wake up to Hong Kong style lion dance music
    - Freestyle: lions wakes up and does freestyle, which can consist or looking around, grooming legs, etc. they start doing this from sitting down, then getting up and continuing a bit more freestyle
    - 7-star
    - 3 bows
    - Modern 1 (typically done in groups of 3 lions): formation is 3 lions standing side by side facing the audience
        - 5 Super snaps: left, right, left, right, middle
        - 2 non-stunt Jumps. center lion jumps in space. left lion jumps to the left, right lion jumps to the right.
        - 6 looks
        - 3 blinks. side lions do cross steps towards the center back into their original position with each blink.
        - 8 heartbeats in place
        - 5 super snaps
        - 7-star
    - Ranchanchan + interactions: lions go off into the audience and interacts with people, objects, etc. 
    - 7-star: used as a "call back beat" to let performers know that interactions are over, and they should head back to the stage area for the rest of routine.
    - Modern 2 (typically done in groups of 3 lions): formation is 1 center lion faces the audience. 2 side lions are facing inwards towards each other, in front of the center lion.
        - 5 Super snaps: left, right, left, right, middle
        - 2 non-stunt Jumps all in place.
        - 6 looks
        - 3 blinks in place
        - 8 heartbeats. center lions stays in place. side lions make small steps to their perspective's left, so they're not in each other's forward plane anymore. they should both remain in front of the center lion
        - 5 super snaps
        - 7-star modified. side lions "frog jump" forward 3 times. center lion jumps in place the first two times, then jumps forward the third time after the two side lions clear out of the way. then, all lions chase their tail until their left shoulder is towards the audience. then, they do cross steps to their right (away from the audience) and end in a pose.
            - note: after this move, the lion that started left will now be on the right side, and the lion that started right will be on the left side - essentially swapping their original positions.
    - Modern 3 (typically done in groups of 3 lions): formation is 3 lions standing side by side facing the audience
        - 5 Super snaps: left, right, left, right, middle
        - 2 non-stunt Jumps all in place.
        - 6 looks
        - 3 blinks in place. blink up, then down, then center.
        - heartbeats (indefinite amount: drummer will watch lions until they're in formation, then continue routine. ideally around 8 heartbeats, but may take longer). center lions stays in place. left lion uses heartbeat steps to walk to be directly in front of center lion. right lion uses heartbeat steps to walk to be directly behind center lion. the 3 lions should now form a straight line facing the audience.
        - 5 super snaps modified. center lions shakes their head in the center. front (originally left) lion bow stances to the left, shaking head 45 degrees in that direction. back (originally right) lion bow stances to the right, shaking head 45 degrees in that direction.
        - 7-star modified. all three lion jump left and snaps head to the right 3 times. front lion does it all 3 times. center lion jumps in place once, then jumps left twice. back lion jumps in place twice, then jumps left once. in the end, formation looks like a diagonal line going from front left to back right. after third jump, lions chamber their left leg and goes down into a drop stance facing the audience, and grooms their left leg, then ends iwht a pose.
    - Scrolls: lions get up from their drop stance. there should be scrolls placed in front them (sometimes all lions, sometimes only some). lions then inspect the scrolls quickly then pick up the scroll with their mouth. if performers are capable, they jump into a stack, then present the scroll. after a few seconds, they drop the scroll down and exit their stack.
    - freestyle transition: small in-place freestyle time between scrolls and next part of routine
    - End of 7-star: 3 super snaps + head spin
    - 3 bows + human bow: finish three bows. then exit lion and bow as a human performer.

- Routines can vary based on stage space and time allotted. Some routines change the order, cut out a modern or two, start with ranchanchan instead of hong kong wake up, etc.
- depending on the performers' capabilities, they might do stunts during the performance. for example, they might do a kick out instead of a head spin at the end of modern 1
